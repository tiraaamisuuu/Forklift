# RIFT-v1: fixed-budget teacher-position selection prototype

Date: 2026-10-13. Status: exploratory, runnable planning prototype; no deep
teacher labels, training jobs or matches were launched for this design.

**Original-to-Forklift design, not an established claim of literature novelty.**
RIFT means *rare-feature and instability-focused teaching*. Selective labeling,
disagreement, budgeted querying, coverage and priority sampling all have clear
precedents. The research opportunity is a controlled chess/NNUE study, not a
new name for active learning. No engine strength improvement is claimed here.

## Hypothesis and implementation

At equal teacher-search and GPU-training budgets, spending deep labels on a
mixture of rare HalfKP features, unstable shallow searches and disagreeing
evaluators may train a stronger compact network than uniform position sampling.
The implementation is `scripts/nnue/rift_sampling.py`; it uses only Python's
standard library. It emits a proposed label allocation and never starts a UCI
engine or trains a model. `tests/rift_sampling_tests.py` exercises its contracts.

There are three reversible policy choices, all exposed in the saved plan:

1. Rarity is the mean of `1 / sqrt(1 + frequency(feature))` over a position's
   unique active features. Frequencies count eligible, deduplicated training
   positions only. Both perspectives are represented with disjoint feature-ID
   ranges: white 0..40959, black 40960..81919. This is a smoothed coverage proxy,
   not a measure of statistical uncertainty or expected Elo gain.
2. Instability is the largest absolute adjacent score change in the supplied
   cheap teacher-search sequence. Disagreement is the spread between supplied
   evaluator scores. Scores must be finite centipawns in the same side-to-move
   convention; saturated +/-32000 mate sentinels are rejected. The caller must
   exclude any other converted mate scores and preserve depth/node information
   in the candidate-generation manifest. No residual against future deep labels
   is allowed as a selection input.
3. Each signal is mapped to eligible-pool empirical midranks. Real zero remains
   zero; a constant positive signal maps to one. This makes the three channels
   comparable without letting an extreme centipawn outlier dominate their sum.
   Default weights are equal thirds. RIFT ranks weighted utility per requested
   deep-label node; rarity-only ranks rarity per node. The random control ranks
   by a seed-derived SHA256 key. Equal-score ties also use that key.

The allocator is greedy: select the next eligible item that fits the remaining
budget and the source-game cap; otherwise skip it. Default game cap is one;
zero disables it. This controls one source of within-game correlation, but does
not ensure semantic/tactical diversity. It is **not** an optimal knapsack solver,
does not guarantee exhaustive spending and is not a calibrated model of label
value. A seed changes ties, not the score weights or features.

## Fixed-budget contract

`teacherNodeLimit` is a cap on **planned UCI node allocations**. Every candidate
pool row's `screen_nodes` is charged first, even if that row is a duplicate,
held out or unselected. `label_nodes` is a fresh, additional teacher search:
the downstream runner must clear hash before each deep search and cannot count
the screening nodes toward that search. The budget ledger records screening,
deep labeling, total allocations and unallocated nodes separately. A pool whose
screening cost already exceeds the cap is rejected, even if selection is empty.

Every screening allocation, deep-label allocation and overall teacher budget
must fit unsigned 64-bit UCI node accounting: at most `2**64 - 1`
(18446744073709551615). Screening and overall allocations may be zero; each
deep-label allocation must be positive. The ceiling is included in saved budget
ledgers, and oversize integers are validation errors rather than converted to
floating-point costs. Seed and provenance IDs are not constrained by this node
ceiling. Scores/weights that cannot be represented as finite Python floats are
also rejected cleanly; the +/-32000 score-sentinel guard remains stricter.

Explicit holdout files contain only descriptors and already-existing guard
keys, not new searches. Their costs are not charged to this selection run. They
must therefore not be used to hide newly acquired teacher queries. Their scores
are never consulted for ranking, rarity counts or normalization.

**This is a planner, not an executed-compute guarantee.** UCI engines can overshoot
a requested node limit. A real experiment must save returned node counts,
teacher executable/options/hash, screening-generation costs, elapsed CPU/GPU
time and completion state. CPU costs for feature/evaluator scoring and GPU
training are separate resources, not free because they use no teacher nodes.
No equal-actual-compute claim can be made from this plan alone.

For the first real ablation, keep every selected position's `label_nodes`
identical. Variable costs are supported for subsequent label-depth studies,
but utility-per-cost is only a heuristic; lower-cost labels may have lower
quality. Compare policies under one common candidate pool and screening budget
before comparing different candidate-generation strategies.

## Candidate format, leakage and lineage

Input is JSONL. A small real-FEN example is:

```json
{"candidate_id":"example-1","source_sha256":"aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa","game_id":1,"ply":20,"split":"training","fen":"4k3/8/8/8/8/8/4P3/4K3 w - - 0 11","screen_nodes":1000,"label_nodes":20000,"teacher_scores_cp":[50,110,70],"evaluator_scores_cp":[50,95]}
```

The source digest in that example is a placeholder, not real provenance.
`source_sha256`, source-local `game_id`, `ply`, split and FEN map to the existing
`generate_dataset.py` JSONL dataset structure. Cheap signal/cost fields are new
sidecar information; this planner does not yet export shards or deep labels.
Existing `score_cp`/`result` targets must **not** be copied into selector input:
unknown fields, including these future-target fields, are rejected.

FEN-derived features follow the current HalfKP-v1 square orientation and relative
piece buckets. The parser validates FEN structure and one king per side, **not
chess legality**. Candidate generation must perform legal-board filtering with
the existing chess tooling. Alternatively, callers may supply `features`,
`position_key` and `leakage_key` directly; then the caller owns those descriptors'
correctness. Any supplied descriptors accompanying a FEN must match its derived
values.

Before computing any selection signals:

- Exclude validation/test rows and every training row sharing their whole-game
  identity. Source hashes namespace numeric game IDs. This source/game identity
  is **always** honored; optional fingerprint metadata can never replace it.
- For copied games across source files, provide `game_fingerprint`: a canonical
  SHA256 over starting FEN plus the full mainline move sequence. Produce it for
  training and holdout records where possible. Union-find grouping joins
  source/game identities sharing a fingerprint, so all same-source rows and
  linked cross-source copies share exclusion and game-cap decisions even when
  some rows lack fingerprints. A fingerprint bridge is needed for every
  cross-source copy group; completely absent metadata cannot establish that
  link. Conflicting fingerprints for one source/game identity are rejected as
  inconsistent provenance, not treated as different games.
- Exclude training rows matching a held-out NNUE input: placement plus side to
  move. This intentionally ignores clocks, rights and en-passant state, which
  are not current network inputs; it is stricter than engine-position equality.
- Deduplicate engine-position keys (placement, side, canonical rights and EP)
  by lexical candidate ID. Reject duplicate candidate IDs. The NNUE-input guard
  does not itself collapse all within-training positions with different rights.

The held-out descriptors are permitted solely to prevent overlap. Their labels
and selection signals must not be used for ranking or tuning. Permanent test
data remain evaluation-only; weights cannot be tuned against test performance.
Exact input exclusion does not prevent similar openings or nearby positions
from appearing in both sets. Group splits by canonical full-game identity and
consider opening-family/source/time partitions in the actual study.

The plan preserves each selected source/game/ply/FEN and candidate-record hash;
canonical pool, held-out guard descriptor and training feature-frequency hashes;
canonical game-group mapping hash and each selected row's group representative;
raw/normalized signals; seed/tie key; weights; budget ledger; and all exclusions.
CLI output also records the exact input-file and selector-file hashes. External
teacher/network/source-license manifests must be preserved separately. Merely
claiming a source hash does not prove that an input file really came from it.

## Reproduce the plumbing ablation

```powershell
python scripts/nnue/rift_sampling.py --demo
python tests/rift_sampling_tests.py
python scripts/nnue/rift_sampling.py --candidates candidate-signals.jsonl --holdout holdout-guards.jsonl --teacher-budget 20000000 --method rift --seed 20261013 --output rift-plan.json
```

`--output` creates a new file exclusively and refuses to overwrite evidence.
Without it, output is JSON on stdout. Use `--method random` and `--method rarity`
to create the other plans with the same input, seed, cap and total allocation.
The `--demo` switch runs all three methods; it does not start a real experiment.

The toy pool has 24 synthetic descriptors, 100 screening nodes each, and 1000
deep-label nodes per selected item. All methods select eight items and allocate
exactly 10400 nodes: 2400 screening + 8000 labeling. At seed 20261013, handcrafted
payoff sums are random 18, rarity 24, RIFT 29. **The hidden payoff was deliberately
made to align with the proposed signals.** It is not passed to the selector, but
its design makes the comparison illustrative, not evidence for usefulness. No
training accuracy, chess result, Elo, teacher label or inference speed was
measured. The demo shows deterministic budgeted behavior and functioning
ablations, nothing stronger.

## Primary-source overlap scan

Sources inspected on 2026-10-13; this is a scoped starting scan, not an exhaustive
novelty assessment. No copied implementation was used.

- Seung, Opper and Sompolinsky, *Query by Committee* (1992), selects queries
  according to learner disagreement. Forklift's evaluator-spread signal is
  related motivation, not the same formal committee construction or guarantee.
  [Author institution record and DOI](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/query-by-committee/).
- Settles, Craven and Friedland, *Active Learning with Real Annotation Costs*
  (2008), studies variable labeling costs rather than treating fewer labels as
  automatically cheaper. This directly overlaps the budget-ledger motivation.
  [Author-hosted paper](https://burrsettles.com/pub/settles.nips08ws.pdf).
- Donmez and Carbonell, *Proactive Learning: Cost-Sensitive Active Learning with
  Multiple Imperfect Oracles* (2008), combines budget constraints and imperfect,
  nonuniform-cost oracles. Different teacher budgets/depths should not be
  presented as a wholly new active-learning premise.
  [Author institution paper](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~pinard/Papers/cikm0613-donmez.pdf).
- Sener and Savarese, *Active Learning for Convolutional Neural Networks: A
  Core-Set Approach* (ICLR 2018), studies representative batch selection. RIFT's
  feature rarity is a simple coverage heuristic, not their geometric algorithm.
  [Paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=H1aIuk-RW).
- Schaul et al., *Prioritized Experience Replay* (ICLR 2016), prioritizes stored
  experience instead of replaying it uniformly. It is relevant to future
  residual/priority sampling, although this prototype queries new teacher labels
  and does not implement replay or its bias-correction mechanism.
  [Author paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05952).
- Stockfish's official NNUE trainer already supports random position skipping,
  smart/WLD filtering, early-ply filtering and configurable piece-count/ply
  distribution shaping. Data selection and mixture shaping are established in
  this domain. Forklift's selection rule requires direct comparison with such
  simpler controls, not only a naive random baseline.
  [Official source](https://github.com/official-stockfish/nnue-pytorch/blob/master/data_loader/config.py).

The specific signal mixture, audits and input guard are new code in Forklift.
Whether a substantive methodological contribution survives a chess-specific
literature review and controlled experiments remains an open question.

## Next experiment and rejection criteria

Freeze one source/game-disjoint pool, one teacher, one student architecture,
one objective and one GPU-step budget. Predeclare random, rarity-only, full
RIFT, instability-only and disagreement-only arms. Reuse one honest cheap screen
for all arms and charge its full cost in every arm. First compare equal-depth
labels; adaptive label depth is a separate factor, not a simultaneous change.
Use multiple independent training seeds. Log candidate and selected slices,
feature coverage, actual teacher nodes, wall time and model hashes.

Primary outcome: equal-resource paired-match Elo against a frozen champion,
with a predeclared stopping/gating rule. Held-out WDL Brier score, MAE, sign
accuracy, inference throughput and coverage are explanatory outcomes. A training
loss improvement alone is not an engine-strength result. No new match campaign
is started by this prototype.

Reject or revise the policy if it consumes more compute, reduces holdout quality
or tactical robustness, or fails to beat appropriate baselines across seeds.
High instability can mean irreducible/search noise, disagreement can reflect
different score scales, and rarity can overselect outliers or malformed states.
Depth changes can alter semantic reliability without representing information
gain. Deterministic top-ranked selection also changes the training distribution;
investigate a predeclared random-exploration mixture and importance weighting
before interpreting fitted losses as population estimates. Those mechanisms,
teacher execution, scalable streaming selection and shard integration are not
implemented in this first prototype.
